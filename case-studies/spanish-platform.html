02 / Learning problem & audience

Keep the learning connected between sessions.

My adult Spanish learners have different starting points and support needs. In a live lesson I can explain a prompt, adjust the challenge, or discuss an error. Between sessions, learners need a clear next step and support they can use independently.

I designed the platform around three needs: assign relevant practice to each learner, connect explanations to active use of Spanish, and bring independent work back into the teaching conversation.

03 / Learning objectives & design approach

Match the activity to the language goal.

I organize practice around a progression from understanding language in context to using it with less support. The objective determines both the activity and how the response is reviewed.

Objective → activity → feedback

Learning objective Activity design Feedback or assessment
  • Recognize target vocabulary and language patterns.
    Contextual explanations followed by closed-answer practice.
    Reference-answer checking for constrained responses.
  • Interpret spoken Spanish.
    Listening activities with the support available for that task.
    Activity feedback and discussion of misunderstandings during teaching.
  • Use Spanish for a communicative purpose.
    Scenario responses and guided role-play, moving toward more open production.
    Teacher review in live lessons; selected AI feedback in independent activities.

Diagnostic and final tasks help frame what to practise and revisit. Completing an activity records participation; demonstrating language use requires examining the response and the support needed.

04 / Learner experience

A clear next step, with a route back to review.

The dashboard brings together assigned materials, homework, and previous attempts. The sequence below summarizes my instructional approach; the activities and their order vary by lesson.

  1. Encounter language in context
    Explanations and listening give vocabulary and patterns meaning before the learner is asked to use them.
  2. Practise with support
    Interactive exercises, cues, and hints let the learner rehearse language with a more constrained response.
  3. Use it to communicate
    Scenario-based practice and role-play ask the learner to choose language for a purpose. In live sessions, I am the conversation partner.
  4. Review and revisit
    Feedback, attempt history, and vocabulary review support independent practice. I review student work to decide what needs attention next.
05 / Teacher-facing content authoring

Design the content and the route through it.

I independently designed the pedagogical requirements, instructional activities, personalized paths, and teacher-facing workflows. The custom authoring tools connect content preparation with assignment and review.

  1. Create and edit. Prepare lessons and activities around the learner’s language needs; revise instructions, content, and practice.
  2. Organize and sequence. Arrange activities and configure learning sequences, choosing the support that prepares a learner for a more open task.
  3. Assign and personalize. Assign content to individual students and manage their paths, connecting the live lesson with relevant homework.
  4. Review and respond. Examine student work, intervene where needed, and use it to plan the next session.

This workflow reduces fragmentation between lesson preparation, homework, and follow-up: I can plan the next teaching decision around the learner’s work.

06 / AI integration & human oversight

Choose feedback for the kind of response.

Open-ended language can be appropriate in several forms. I use AI selectively where a predefined answer is too restrictive, while keeping teacher judgment part of the learning workflow.

Deterministic answer checking

Checks constrained activities against reference answers, where the expected response can be defined in advance.

Selected AI-assisted feedback

Supports selected written, voice, and dialogue activities where wording can vary and a contextual response is useful.

Learner self-assessment

Offers opportunities to reflect on a response and identify language to revisit.

Teacher review and intervention

I interpret errors in context, review student work, and decide whether to correct feedback or provide more support.

AI-assisted content generation

Helps draft learning material; I define the objective, activity, and instructional sequence.

Example from the voice-feedback design

Clarify the question before giving the answer.

The feedback prompt includes a self-modelling move: “¿Su nombre?” → “Yo soy María. ¿Y usted?” This gives a usable language cue while leaving the learner to supply their own name.

AI is intended to reformulate around the current turn and learner attempt. A fixed answer key would offer less flexibility; the trade-off is that the generated feedback needs teacher validation.

07 / Real-world use & evidence

Part of my teaching practice for over a year.

Approximately 20 students have used the platform over more than one year, in instructor-led sessions and for independent learning between lessons. Learner activities, personalized assignments, teacher content tools, and work review form an operational teaching workflow.

Regular teaching use gives me practical experience of selecting material, supporting learners, and reviewing their work. Measured learning effectiveness has not yet been established.

08 / Reflection, limitations & next steps

Make the learning signals more useful.

Clarify progress. Completion logic is inconsistent in some areas. I would distinguish opening, attempting, and finishing an activity so progress gives learners and teachers a clearer next step.

Give lessons a shared structure. Content organization varies. A consistent core sequence would make independent practice easier to follow and authoring easier to maintain, with room for individual needs.

Validate AI feedback. I would compare representative responses with teacher judgments, including acceptable alternatives, unsuitable corrections, and failures.

For the next evaluation, I would observe assignment-to-review tasks and use a new communicative scenario with a later follow-up, recording the support required. This would examine usability and language transfer alongside completion.
