01 — The problem

The learner needs to practise repair, not perfection

A Russian-speaking expat in Spain has to book a doctor, decline a contract, explain a leak to the neighbour. She opens the app, the phone rings, and she has to produce spoken Spanish — fast, without a metalanguage safety net.

The exercise is intended to practise communication repair. The goal is not a grammatically pristine sentence; the goal is the next move after the first one collapses.

The design treats the model as an in-character conversation partner. It aims to make clarification and reformulation part of the situation, with support when the learner stalls.

03 — Intended response constraints

Keep responses focused on the learner’s task

A working definition

A pedagogically sound AI response is one that simultaneously —

  1. respects the learner's L2 production zone: no metalanguage, no L1 leak into target-language turns;
  2. stays within the persona's communicative range — a line a real Spaniard might actually say;
  3. advances the task or scaffolds it through a defined repair move;
  4. is verifiable against a finite list of constraints, not “feels right”.

Proposed validation checklist

I would add these checks to a response-validation layer. Hard checks would block unsuitable output; soft checks would flag it for revision or review. Prompt instructions alone cannot ensure that a response meets these requirements.

Three tiers of evidence

I would evaluate the exercise at three levels, using activity data alongside teacher review and new communicative tasks.

01 — Engagement

Did the learner stay in the call?

Time-on-task, completion rate, voluntary repeat. Measures attention, not learning.

02 — Performance

Can the learner complete this task now?

Turns to completion, how far the hint ladder had to escalate, whether L1 fallback was triggered.

03 — Transfer

Can the learner do it somewhere else?

Same grammar zone, different scenario and lexicon — doctor's appointment vs. asking a neighbour to lower the noise. Success in one familiar scenario does not, by itself, demonstrate transfer.

Review response quality alongside learner performance: a fluent AI response may still give unsuitable language or support for the task.
04 — The hint ladder

Five repair moves when the learner stalls

The documented design proposes five repair techniques informed by negotiation-of-meaning principles. A different technique on each rung — three identical hints in a row is not a ladder.

T1
Simpler synonym
¿La tarjeta sanitaria?→¿La tarjeta verde, la del médico?
T2
Concrete examples
¿Cómo se llama?→¿Iván? ¿María? ¿Pedro?
T3
Split the compound
¿Nombre y apellidos?→Primero el nombre.
T4
Close the question
¿Cuándo?→¿Hoy o mañana?
T5
Self-modelling
¿Su nombre?→Yo soy María. ¿Y usted?

Escalation: first attempt uses T1, T2, or T3 (open). Second attempt moves to T4 or T5 (more scaffolded). Third attempt combines or models almost the full answer — still in character.

05 — Design trade-offs & next steps

Improve response checks and recovery

Move from prompt instructions to automated checks. Word limits, character consistency, and language restrictions are intended behavior. The proposed validation layer would make selected requirements testable before a response reaches the learner.

Handle variation in learner speech. Regex extraction can miss natural variation in names, times, and other details. I would add fallback steps that ask for clarification when essential information is unclear.

Validate responses before audio playback. I would prioritize JSON schema checks and a recovery path for malformed model responses before passing them to text-to-speech.

Evaluate learning separately. Response accuracy, language-level suitability, and transfer to a new task remain priorities for evaluation.
